# Review of the claimed stack proof

The claim is false. The operation rules are trusted axioms over untyped closures: client checking proves consequences of those rules, not that the implementation satisfies them. Smallest counterexample: `top(push(1, empty))` typechecks as a number under the claimed rules, but push returns `empty`, so it cannot deliver 1; depending on the empty closure, it fails or returns some unrelated value. No execution result is asserted here.

Repair push to construct a fresh persistent node containing the new value and predecessor. Prefer a concrete indexed representation with constructor introduction/elimination rules and typecheck empty, push, top, and pop bodies, including a rejected growth body returning its input. Alternatively, audit an untyped representation and explicitly mark operation rules as trusted. In either case test and reason about `top(push(x,s)) = x` and `pop(push(x,s)) = s`, old aliases, and multi-element order. Structural indices constrain admissible length shapes, not ordering or those equations. Persistence and purity require analysis of mutation, aliasing and captured values; termination requires a separate argument. Tests give examples, not universal proofs. All judgments remain relative to the active datatype axioms and runtime.
