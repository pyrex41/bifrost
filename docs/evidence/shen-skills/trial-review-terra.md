# Independent review of held-out battery

## Bottom line

**Exclude terra-original from a clean original-versus-revised comparison.** Its recorded session searched source files in external repository checkouts, contrary to the prompt, and its parser is explicitly incomplete. It remains useful as a documented, contaminated partial run—not as a clean original-condition result. The other three cells are eligible for a *qualified descriptive* comparison: I found no recorded cross-condition/checkout reads, delegation, checker alteration, or fabricated checker run in their sessions. Eligibility is not proof of sandbox compliance or general superiority. Sol-original and sol-revised both substantially meet the six requested tasks; terra-revised also substantially meets them, with some weaker observations noted below. Green counts and exit 0 alone do not establish completion.

I inspected `tasks.md`, all 24 final `answers/*/SKILL.md` files, all four reports, metadata, recorded session tool calls and their relevant results, and the independent-check summaries. I recomputed the prompt, recorded input and answer SHA-256s against metadata: all recorded files matched. This verifies those files as recorded, not the entire environment. I did not independently verify checker/runtime binary hashes or rerun the checker; the separate logs are evidence of runs, not my own execution.

Session calls substantiate the reported checker results: sol-original 8 then 10 blocks; sol-revised 10; terra-original first 8 with two failures, then 8, 9 and 10 green; terra-revised 8 green. Independent-check logs end respectively 10, 10, 10, and 8 blocks, zero failed. Different fence counts, expected rejection fences and prose-only review make block totals unsuitable as task scores. Assertions in the delivered code generally raise errors; expected-rejection blocks are intentionally discarded. No evidence that assertions were deliberately neutralized or answers replaced by hard-coded derived result sets. Error-catching parser tests accept any parse-time exception, not a specific diagnostic.

The terra-original session actually ran `rg -n 'absvector|vector->|<-vector|\(vector ' input-skills "$BIFROST_PORTS_ROOT" -g '*.shen' | head -35`; the environment in the other recorded session identifies that root as `<HOME>/projects`, and the returned output includes `<HOME>/projects/ShenOSKernel-43.1/sources/...` and other checkout source paths. This is a real external-source read, not merely adapter selection. The report discloses the command and earlier failed check, but does not cure the violation. The instructions explicitly permitted inspecting the supplied runtime, not searching other checkouts. Other cells' recorded reads and shell calls stay with supplied inputs, trial files and permitted runtime/checker access; this is an observation of the logs, not a guarantee about unobserved access. No delegation is visible. I found no invented execution result in the prose reviews; each states the unknown closure library outcome conditionally. Reports' checker claims match recorded results.

## Per-cell assessment

### sol-original — eligible, with stated trust limits

- **Vocabulary:** nested finite product over authoritative token-sequence alternatives; teal extends the source, not the generated set. Exact four/six and uniqueness assertions, and epsilon on either side/both, distinguish an empty alternative from no alternatives. Inductive completeness and rendering-injectivity assumptions are appropriately stated.
- **History:** concrete successor-indexed list introduction/elimination rules and checked bodies, including double and intentionally false growth. Three expected type errors cover both empty reads and false growth. Old/new observations and equality of the reconstructed predecessor support persistence and predecessor preservation; the index itself does not prove those semantic laws.
- **Prerequisites:** untyped `defprolog` plus trusted `q.raw` declaration, then typed deduplication and set/cardinality checks. Ground edge endpoints and seen-list membership make Boolean negation meaningful; finite simple-path enumeration terminates and covers reachability without returning the root. This is an explicit trust boundary, not a checked Prolog implementation.
- **Parser:** typed `defcc` actions, separate term/factor levels, left folds and evaluator. Exact AST assertions for both repeated operators show associativity rather than relying on associative arithmetic; malformed/trailing tests exercise `compile` consumption. The number-guard and conditional typing rules are trusted extensions, not a declared untyped parse result. General recognition and termination are not type theorems.
- **Transition/review:** explicit balance and captured numeric fee; independent configurations, replay, overdraft and predecessor checks; separate vector closure checks sharing and allocation. Review identifies the false type axiom, counterexample and repair without claiming an unrun result. Behavioral guarantees depend on bodies and runtime, not arrow types.

### sol-revised — eligible, with stated trust limits

The same task obligations are substantially covered by independently inspected code. Vocabulary uses `""` as epsilon with a join that avoids padding and an explicit empty-choice-list test. Indexed history checks operation bodies and the three required negative cases; replacement reuses the predecessor and observes the old alias. Prerequisites use a typed visited worklist rather than Prolog, with a finite catalog, ground Boolean membership and exact closures. Parser tails compose left-accumulating functions; exact trees check both operator associativities and `compile` is tested against trailing tokens. A negative semantic-action fence is additional evidence, while number/branch rules are still trusted. Transition uses explicit balance and fee closures plus a separately allocated vector counter; review does not invent a library run. Runtime, open datatype rules and finite tests remain assumptions, as the report says.

### terra-original — **ineligible for clean comparison**; incomplete

The external `rg` above violates the protocol. Independently of that, `answers/parser/SKILL.md` has only an AST datatype and evaluator: no token parser, checked parser actions, complete-consumption boundary or malformed/trailing tests. Its report explicitly acknowledges this, so the 10-block green result is not a completed six-task battery. The other tasks contain vocabulary/epsilon assertions; checked indexed operations and named negatives; ground finite Prolog traversal with an explicitly trusted declaration; explicit-balance account plus an untyped counter; and an honest prose review. The account body does not mutate its numeric predecessor, but unlike the other cells its assertions do not explicitly bind and check an unchanged predecessor balance, as requested. The history persistence check is also weaker than retaining and observing an old alias after a new operation. Nonnegative fee/amount is a caller assumption, not enforced. These observations do not rescue eligibility.

### terra-revised — eligible for qualified comparison

Vocabulary is a source-driven Cartesian product with exact results, uniqueness and epsilon checks. History has checked structural operations and the three required negative fences; its persistence test retains `S` and observes both predecessors, but checks their newest values rather than directly asserting equality of the entire predecessor. The checked body visibly reuses the same tail, so the evidence is narrower than a direct predecessor-equality assertion, not evidence of an incorrect implementation. Prerequisites use a finite visited worklist, exclude the origin, and assert exact closures; no unground Prolog negation is involved. Parser has typed AST-producing actions, precedence nesting and left-accumulating tails; its mixed exact tree exercises repeated plus and times, and it tests malformed and trailing input. Its logical number/if rules remain trusted and the tests do not establish full language correctness. Transition uses captured fee and explicit balance, tests replay and independent policies, and separately tests alias sharing and a fresh counter; nonnegative inputs are assumed rather than enforced. Review correctly distinguishes asserted operation types from checked implementations and makes no execution claim.

## Interpretation limits

This is a fresh review of user-requested SOL/TERRA models, **not a blinded evaluation**. There is one run per model/condition, no demonstrated sandbox, no randomization or replication, and the six tasks are correlated around supplied Shen skills and shared primitives. Original and revised inputs differ substantially, including linked reference material; a descriptive outcome cannot isolate why an answer differed. Metadata and logs cannot rule out unrecorded access or hidden state, and common checker/runtime defects could affect all cells. Type-system extensions, untyped boundaries, engine semantics, groundness and finite graph assumptions must be kept separate from checked bodies and behavioral examples. No population-level performance or causal improvement claim is warranted, and no clean terra original-versus-revised pair remains after exclusion.
